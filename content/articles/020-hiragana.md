Hiragana is the first script every Japanese child learns, and it is the one you will read most. Every word in Japanese can be written in hiragana, even when it is usually written in kanji. Children's books are written almost entirely in it.

There are 46 basic characters. That may sound like a lot, but they follow a grid that makes them far easier to learn than 46 random shapes.

## The grid

Every row starts with a consonant, and every column is one of the five vowels: a, i, u, e, o. Read across a row and you get that consonant with each vowel in turn.

::: kana
title: Vowels and the k, s, t rows
あ a | い i | う u | え e | お o
か ka | き ki | く ku | け ke | こ ko
さ sa | し shi | す su | せ se | そ so
た ta | ち chi | つ tsu | て te | と to
:::

Notice the three surprises: し is *shi*, ち is *chi* and つ is *tsu*. Japanese does not have the sounds *si*, *ti* or *tu*, so these are the closest natural sounds.

::: kana
title: The n, h, m rows
な na | に ni | ぬ nu | ね ne | の no
は ha | ひ hi | ふ fu | へ he | ほ ho
ま ma | み mi | む mu | め me | も mo
:::

ふ sits between *fu* and *hu*: blow gently through your lips rather than biting your lip as in English *f*.

::: kana
title: The y, r, w rows and ん
や ya | - | ゆ yu | - | よ yo
ら ra | り ri | る ru | れ re | ろ ro
わ wa | - | - | - | を o
ん n | - | - | - | -
:::

The y row has only three sounds, and the w row only two. を is pronounced *o* and is used almost only as a particle, which you will meet later. ん is the only character that is just a consonant.

The Japanese *r* is a light tap of the tongue, halfway between an English r, l and d. Say "ladder" quickly in an American accent and the middle sound is close.

## Words you can already read

With these characters you can read real words. Try them before looking at the romaji.

::: vocab
- さくら | cherry blossom | noun
- すし | sushi | noun
- ねこ | cat | noun
- いぬ | dog | noun
- あなた | you | pronoun
- ありがとう | thank you | expression
:::

## How to learn them

- **A row a day.** Ten rows means you can read all of hiragana within two weeks without strain.
- **Read, then write.** Writing a character a few times helps you notice its shape, but reading is the skill that matters most.
- **Use them at once.** Every article in this paper uses hiragana. Reading real words fixes the characters in memory far better than drilling a chart.

::: callout
title: Look-alikes
A few pairs are easy to mix up: さ and き, は and ほ, ぬ and め, る and ろ, わ and れ. Look at the endings: ぬ and る have a small loop at the bottom, め and ろ do not.
:::

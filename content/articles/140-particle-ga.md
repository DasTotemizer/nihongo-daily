The particle が (ga) marks the thing that fits the description: *this* one, not any other. Where は introduces a topic, が identifies. The difference is easiest to see with questions.

## Answering "who?"

::: dialogue
Aya: だれが {学生|がくせい}？ // Who is the student?
Ben: ジョンが {学生|がくせい} だ。 // John is the student.
:::

The question word だれ (who) takes が, and so does the answer. Ben is not making a general comment about John. He is picking John out of everyone else.

::: breakdown
ja: ジョン が {学生|がくせい} だ。
en: John is the student. (It's John who is the student.)
- ジョン = John
- が = identifier: picks out the one that fits
- {学生|がくせい}だ = is a student
:::

## Question words never take は

Words like だれ (who), なに (what) and どれ (which one) are asking you to identify something, so they use が.

::: mistake
wrong: だれ は {先生|せんせい}？
right: だれ が {先生|せんせい}？
why: You cannot make "who" the topic, because nobody knows who it is yet. Question words are identified with が.
:::

## Compare the two

::: example
ja: ジョン は {学生|がくせい} だ。
en: John is a student. (As for John, he's a student.)
note: A comment about John, our topic.
:::

::: example
ja: ジョン が {学生|がくせい} だ。
en: John is the student. (John, not anyone else.)
note: Picking out John as the one who fits.
:::

In English, the difference often lives in your voice: "*John* is the student." Japanese puts it in the particle.

::: callout
title: Where you will meet が most
が appears all the time with words of liking, wanting and ability, such as {好|す}き (liked) and ほしい (wanted). You will meet those soon, and it helps to remember that が marks the thing that is liked or wanted.
:::

The full story of は and が takes a while to absorb, and that is normal. Our feature on **は versus が** looks at it from a few more angles once you have this lesson under your belt.

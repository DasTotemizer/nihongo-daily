しまう on its own means *put away* or *finish*. After a て form, it means the action is completely done, and often that you wish it hadn't happened.

## Regret and accidents

::: example
ja: {財布|さいふ} を {忘|わす}れて しまった。
en: I went and forgot my wallet.
:::

::: example
ja: ケーキ を {全部|ぜんぶ} {食|た}べて しまった。
en: I ended up eating the whole cake.
:::

## Doing something completely

::: example
ja: {宿題|しゅくだい} を {先|さき} に やって しまおう。
en: Let's get the homework done first.
:::

## The casual forms

::: vocab
- {忘|わす}れちゃった | I forgot | てしまった → ちゃった
- {飲|の}んじゃった | I drank it | でしまった → じゃった
- {行|い}っちゃう | will go off | てしまう → ちゃう
:::

::: dialogue
A: あれ、{電車|でんしゃ} は? // Hey, where's the train?
B: もう {行|い}っちゃった よ。 // It's already gone.
:::

::: callout
title: Is it always bad?
Not always. てしまう can just mean *finished*. But in most everyday sentences it carries a small sigh.
:::

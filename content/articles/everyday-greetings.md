Greetings in Japan are short, frequent and important. You greet neighbours, shop staff and colleagues, often with a small bow.

## Through the day

::: vocab
- おはよう ございます | good morning | polite
- こんにちは | hello, good afternoon | daytime
- こんばんは | good evening | evening
- おやすみなさい | good night | before bed
:::

::: callout
title: The は in こんにちは
こんにちは and こんばんは end in は, but it is said *wa*. They were once longer sentences, and that は is the particle は.
:::

With friends and family, おはよう on its own is fine. ございます makes it polite.

## Coming and going

::: vocab
- いってきます | I'm off | said when leaving home
- いってらっしゃい | see you later | said to the one leaving
- ただいま | I'm home | said when arriving home
- おかえりなさい | welcome back | said to the one arriving
:::

These four come in pairs. They are said every single day in Japanese homes, and often at work too.

::: dialogue
A: ただいま。 // I'm home.
B: おかえり。 // Welcome back.
:::

## And goodbye?

さようなら exists, but it sounds rather final, like a long farewell. Friends usually say じゃあね or また{明日|あした} (see you tomorrow).

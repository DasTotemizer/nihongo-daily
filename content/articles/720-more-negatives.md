## ないで: without doing

Add で to the negative form to say you did one thing without doing another.

::: example
ja: {朝|あさ} ご{飯|はん} を {食|た}べないで {学校|がっこう} に {行|い}った。
en: I went to school without eating breakfast.
:::

::: example
ja: {傘|かさ} を {持|も}たないで {出|で}かけた。
en: I went out without an umbrella.
:::

## ず and ずに: the written version

Replace ない with ず (or ずに). It means the same, but sounds more formal. する becomes せず.

::: example
ja: {何|なに} も {言|い}わず に {帰|かえ}った。
en: He went home without saying anything.
:::

::: example
ja: {勉強|べんきょう} せず に {試験|しけん} を {受|う}けた。
en: I took the exam without studying.
:::

## ぬ and ん: the old negatives

ぬ is a classical negative you will see in proverbs and set phrases. In some dialects and polite speech, ません ends in the same ん.

::: example
ja: {知|し}らぬ {間|ま} に {寝|ね}て いた。
en: I fell asleep before I knew it.
:::

::: example
ja: すみません。{知|し}りません。
en: Sorry. I don't know. (ません ends in this ん)
:::

::: callout
title: Two meanings of ないで
ないでください means *please don't*, and ないで on its own means *without*. Both come from the same negative て form.
:::

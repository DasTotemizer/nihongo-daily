English uses *you* for everyone. Japanese prefers names. When you talk to someone, you normally say their family name with a title, even when you are speaking to them directly.

## Titles after a name

::: vocab
- {田中|たなか}さん | Mr, Ms or Mrs Tanaka | the everyday title
- {田中|たなか}{様|さま} | Tanaka-sama | very polite, for customers and letters
- {田中|たなか}{先生|せんせい} | Tanaka-sensei | teachers, doctors and other experts
- ゆきちゃん | little Yuki | children and close friends
- {健|けん}くん | Ken-kun | boys and younger men
:::

Never add さん to your own name. Titles show respect to others, so using one for yourself sounds odd.

::: breakdown
ja: {田中|たなか}さん は {明日|あした} {来|き}ます か。
en: Are you coming tomorrow, Mr Tanaka?
- {田中|たなか}さんは = as for Mr Tanaka: used instead of *you*
- {明日|あした} = tomorrow
- {来|き}ますか = will come?
:::

## Words for "you"

::: vocab
- あなた | you | polite but distant; also how wives may address husbands
- {君|きみ} | you | casual, used by men to friends or juniors
- お{前|まえ} | you | very rough; avoid it
:::

If you know the person's name, use it. If you do not, you can often leave *you* out completely, because the situation makes it clear.

## Words for "I"

::: vocab
- {私|わたし} | I | the safe, polite choice
- {僕|ぼく} | I | casual and soft, mostly boys and men
- {俺|おれ} | I | very casual and rough, mostly men
- あたし | I | casual, mostly women
:::

## Family words

Japanese has two sets of family words: humble ones for your own family, and polite ones for someone else's.

::: vocab
- {母|はは} / お{母|かあ}さん | my mother / your mother | own / someone else's
- {父|ちち} / お{父|とう}さん | my father / your father | own / someone else's
- {兄|あに} / お{兄|にい}さん | my older brother / your older brother | own / someone else's
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {私|わたし} は {田中|たなか}さん です。
right: {私|わたし} は {田中|たなか} です。
why: Titles are for other people. Introduce yourself with just your name.
:::

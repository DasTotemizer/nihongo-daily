Polite language has three layers. ます and です are the everyday polite level. Above them are **honorific** words ({尊敬語|そんけいご}), which raise up the other person, and **humble** words ({謙譲語|けんじょうご}), which lower yourself. This lesson covers honorifics.

## Special honorific verbs

Some common verbs have their own honorific replacements.

::: vocab
- {行|い}く・{来|く}る・いる → いらっしゃる | go, come, be | honorific
- {言|い}う → おっしゃる | say | honorific
- {食|た}べる・{飲|の}む → {召|め}し{上|あ}がる | eat, drink | honorific
- {見|み}る → ご{覧|らん}になる | see | honorific
- する → なさる | do | honorific
- {知|し}っている → ご{存|ぞん}じだ | know | honorific
:::

Five of these have an irregular ます form: いらっしゃいます, おっしゃいます, なさいます, くださいます and ございます.

::: example
ja: {社長|しゃちょう} は {何時|なんじ} に いらっしゃいます か。
en: What time will the company president come?
:::

::: example
ja: どうぞ {召|め}し{上|あ}がって ください。
en: Please, help yourself.
:::

## The regular pattern: お～になる

For other verbs, put お before the ます stem and add になる.

::: breakdown
ja: {先生|せんせい} は もう お{帰|かえ}り に なりました。
en: The teacher has already gone home.
- お{帰|かえ}り = the stem of {帰|かえ}る, with お
- になりました = honorific ending, past
:::

## Polite requests: お～ください

The same stem pattern with ください makes very polite instructions. You will see them on signs everywhere.

::: example
ja: {少々|しょうしょう} お{待|ま}ち ください。
en: Please wait a moment.
:::

::: callout
title: Never for yourself
Honorific verbs are only for other people. Using them about yourself sounds as if you're praising yourself.
:::

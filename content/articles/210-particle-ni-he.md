The particle に marks a target, the point that something is aimed at. That covers three everyday jobs: where you go, when something happens, and where something is.

## Where you go

::: breakdown
ja: {学校|がっこう} に {行|い}く。
en: (I'm) going to school.
- {学校|がっこう} = school
- に = target: the destination
- {行|い}く = go
:::

::: example
ja: {日本|にほん} に {来|き}た。
en: (I) came to Japan.
:::

::: example
ja: {家|うち} に {帰|かえ}る。
en: (I'm) going home.
:::

## When it happens

Specific times take に: clock times, days of the week, dates.

::: example
ja: {七時|しちじ} に {起|お}きる。
en: (I) get up at seven.
:::

::: example
ja: {日曜日|にちようび} に {映画|えいが} を {見|み}た。
en: (I) watched a film on Sunday.
:::

Relative times, like {今日|きょう} (today), {明日|あした} (tomorrow) and {毎日|まいにち} (every day), do not take に. They are already relative to now.

## Where something is

With いる (for people and animals) and ある (for things), に marks the place where they exist.

::: example
ja: {猫|ねこ} は {部屋|へや} に いる。
en: The cat is in the room.
:::

::: example
ja: {駅|えき} の {前|まえ} に {銀行|ぎんこう} が ある。
en: There is a bank in front of the station.
note: の links {駅|えき} and {前|まえ}: the station's front. You will meet の properly soon.
:::

## へ: the direction

へ is written with the hiragana for *he* but said **e**. It marks a direction, the way you are heading, rather than a destination you reach.

::: example
ja: {東京|とうきょう} へ {行|い}く。
en: (I'm) heading to Tokyo.
:::

With verbs of going and coming, に and へ are usually interchangeable. へ just sounds a little more like "towards".

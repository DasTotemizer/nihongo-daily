Casual speech is not wrong. It's simply what people say to friends. You should understand it, even if you don't use it with strangers.

## Merged sounds

::: vocab
- {行|い}っちゃう | from {行|い}ってしまう | てしまう becomes ちゃう
- {食|た}べてる | from {食|た}べている | the い drops
- {見|み}とく | from {見|み}ておく | ておく becomes とく
- {言|い}わなきゃ | from {言|い}わなければ | must, shortened
- {飲|の}んじゃ だめ | from {飲|の}んでは だめ | では becomes じゃ
:::

## ん instead of の

The explanatory の often becomes ん, and ら or れ sounds before it become ん too.

::: example
ja: {何|なに} して ん の?
en: What are you doing? (from {何|なに}しているの)
:::

::: example
ja: わかん ない。
en: Dunno. (from わからない)
:::

## Dropped particles

Friends often leave out は, を and が when the meaning is clear.

::: example
ja: {昨日|きのう} {映画|えいが} {見|み}た?
en: Did you see the film yesterday?
:::

## Little words at the end

::: vocab
- じゃん | isn't it, you see | {言|い}ったじゃん, I told you
- っけ | was it? | trying to remember: {何時|なんじ}だっけ
- かな | I wonder | {来|く}るかな
- さ | you know | a pause filler, mostly men
- わ | soft emphasis | sometimes used by women
:::

::: dialogue
A: {明日|あした} {何時|なんじ} だっけ? // What time was it tomorrow?
B: {十時|じゅうじ} って {言|い}った じゃん。 // I told you, ten o'clock.
:::

::: callout
title: Know your audience
Use these with friends your own age. At work or with people you don't know, stick to です and ます.
:::

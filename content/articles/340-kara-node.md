English can say *I stayed home because it rained*. Japanese puts the reason first: *it rained, so I stayed home*.

## から: because, so

Put から straight after the reason. With a noun or な-adjective, add だ first.

::: breakdown
ja: {雨|あめ} だ から、{家|いえ} に いる。
en: It's raining, so I'm staying home.
- {雨|あめ}だから = because it's rain
- {家|いえ}にいる = I'm at home
:::

::: example
ja: {時間|じかん} が ない から、タクシー で {行|い}こう。
en: We don't have time, so let's go by taxi.
:::

You can also answer a question with just the reason and から.

::: dialogue
A: どうして {食|た}べない の? // Why aren't you eating?
B: お{腹|なか} が いっぱい だ から。 // Because I'm full.
:::

## ので: a softer because

ので means the same thing, but it sounds more explanatory and polite. With a noun or な-adjective, use なので.

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} は {試験|しけん} なので、{今日|きょう} は {早|はや}く {寝|ね}ます。
en: I have an exam tomorrow, so I'm going to bed early today.
:::

::: callout
title: から or ので?
から states a reason firmly. ので presents it as a natural explanation, so it is often used for excuses and requests at work.
:::

## のに: even though

のに joins two ideas that clash, often with a feeling of disappointment. With a noun or な-adjective, use なのに.

::: example
ja: {勉強|べんきょう} した のに、{試験|しけん} に {落|お}ちた。
en: Even though I studied, I failed the exam.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {学生|がくせい} ので、お{金|かね} が ない。
right: {学生|がくせい} なので、お{金|かね} が ない。
why: After a noun, ので needs な in front of it, just as から needs だ.
:::

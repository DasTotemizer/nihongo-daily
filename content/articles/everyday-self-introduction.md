In Japan, introducing yourself is a small ritual, called {自己紹介|じこしょうかい}. It is short, polite and follows a pattern.

## The shape

1. Open with はじめまして (nice to meet you, literally "for the first time").
2. Say your name with です.
3. Add one or two facts: where you are from, what you do.
4. Close with よろしく お{願|ねが}いします.

::: breakdown
ja: はじめまして。サム です。
en: Nice to meet you. I'm Sam.
- はじめまして = nice to meet you
- サム = the name, in katakana
- です = polite "am"
:::

## Adding facts

::: example
ja: イギリス から {来|き}ました。
en: I'm from the UK. (I came from the UK.)
note: から means "from".
:::

::: example
ja: {会社員|かいしゃいん} です。
en: I'm an office worker.
:::

::: example
ja: {日本語|にほんご} を {勉強|べんきょう} して います。
en: I'm studying Japanese.
:::

## The closing phrase

よろしく お{願|ねが}いします has no neat English version. It means something like "please treat me kindly", or "I look forward to working with you". It is always said at the end.

::: callout
title: Skip the {私|わたし}は
In an introduction, everyone knows you are talking about yourself. サムです is more natural than {私|わたし}はサムです.
:::

The second kind of Japanese adjective ends in い and handles its own grammar. You do not add だ to it, and you do not add な before a noun. It simply sits there and changes its ending.

## Before a noun: nothing extra

::: example
ja: おいしい ケーキ
en: delicious cake
:::

::: example
ja: {高|たか}い {山|やま}
en: a tall mountain
note: {高|たか}い means high or tall, and also expensive.
:::

## As the comment: no だ needed

::: example
ja: この ケーキ は おいしい。
en: This cake is delicious.
:::

Adding だ here, おいしいだ, is a mistake. An い-adjective already works like a complete statement.

## Changing the ending

Here is where the pattern from じゃない pays off. Replace the final い to change the meaning.

::: vocab
- {高|たか}い | is expensive | present
- {高|たか}くない | is not expensive | negative: い becomes くない
- {高|たか}かった | was expensive | past: い becomes かった
- {高|たか}くなかった | was not expensive | past negative
:::

::: breakdown
ja: {昨日|きのう} は {寒|さむ}かった。
en: It was cold yesterday.
- {昨日|きのう}は = as for yesterday
- {寒|さむ}かった = was cold: {寒|さむ}い with い replaced by かった
:::

## The one irregular adjective

いい means good, and it is the only exception. Whenever it conjugates, it borrows the forms of an older word, よい.

::: vocab
- いい | is good | present
- よくない | is not good | negative
- よかった | was good | past
- よくなかった | was not good | past negative
:::

You have probably heard よかった already. On its own it means "that's great" or "what a relief".

::: mistake
wrong: いくない
right: よくない
why: いい always uses よ- when it changes: よくない, よかった.
:::

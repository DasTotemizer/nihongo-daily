You already know とたん for *the moment that*. These three are more literary.

## が{早|はや}いか: no sooner than

The dictionary form plus が{早|はや}いか means the second action happened almost at the same time.

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} は {家|いえ} に {帰|かえ}る が {早|はや}いか、ベッド に {倒|たお}れ{込|こ}んだ。
en: No sooner had he got home than he collapsed onto the bed.
:::

## や{否|いな}や: as soon as

や{否|いな}や, or just や, follows the dictionary form. It is even more formal.

::: breakdown
ja: ベル が {鳴|な}る や {否|いな}や、{生徒|せいと} たち は {教室|きょうしつ} を {飛|と}び{出|だ}した。
en: The moment the bell rang, the students rushed out of the classroom.
- ベルが{鳴|な}る = the bell rings
- や{否|いな}や = as soon as
- {飛|と}び{出|だ}した = rushed out
:::

## そばから: no sooner done than undone

そば means *side*. After a verb, そばから means something keeps being undone as soon as it is done, again and again.

::: example
ja: {片付|かたづ}ける そば から、{子|こ}ども が {散|ち}らかす。
en: As soon as I tidy up, the kids make a mess again.
:::

::: example
ja: {覚|おぼ}えた そば から {忘|わす}れて いく。
en: I forget things as fast as I learn them.
:::

::: callout
title: Telling them apart
が{早|はや}いか and や{否|いな}や describe one dramatic moment. そばから describes something that keeps happening.
:::

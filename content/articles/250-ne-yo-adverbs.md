A plain sentence states a fact. Put ね or よ on the end and it starts to sound like a real conversation.

## ね: "isn't it?"

ね invites the listener to agree. Use it when you both already know, or when you expect them to feel the same.

::: example
ja: {今日|きょう} は {暑|あつ}い ね。
en: It's hot today, isn't it?
:::

::: example
ja: きれい だ ね。
en: It's beautiful, isn't it?
:::

## よ: "you know"

よ tells the listener something they may not know. It adds a little emphasis, like "I'm telling you".

::: example
ja: この {店|みせ} は {安|やす}い よ。
en: This shop is cheap, you know.
:::

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} は {休|やす}み だ よ。
en: Tomorrow is a day off, you know.
:::

## よね: both at once

Together, よね means "right?" You are fairly sure, and checking.

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} {来|く}る よね。
en: You're coming tomorrow, right?
:::

::: callout
title: Go easy on よ
Too much よ can sound pushy, as if you are correcting someone. ね is softer and safer when in doubt.
:::

## Adverbs: how much, how often

Adverbs come before the word they describe.

::: vocab
- とても | very
- ちょっと | a little
- よく | often, well
- たくさん | a lot
- もう | already
- まだ | still, not yet
:::

::: example
ja: とても {面白|おもしろ}い。
en: (It's) very interesting.
:::

::: example
ja: よく {図書館|としょかん} に {行|い}く。
en: (I) often go to the library.
:::

## Making adverbs from adjectives

い-adjectives swap い for く. な-adjectives add に.

::: vocab
- {早|はや}い → {早|はや}く | early → early (adverb) | い → く
- {静|しず}か → {静|しず}かに | quiet → quietly | add に
:::

::: example
ja: {早|はや}く {起|お}きた。
en: (I) got up early.
:::

## Making the potential form

- る-verbs: change る to られる. {食|た}べる becomes {食|た}べられる.
- う-verbs: move the last sound to the *e* column and add る. {飲|の}む becomes {飲|の}める.
- する becomes できる, and {来|く}る becomes {来|こ}られる.

::: vocab
- {見|み}る → {見|み}られる | can see | る-verb
- {話|はな}す → {話|はな}せる | can speak | す → せる
- {行|い}く → {行|い}ける | can go | く → ける
- {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}てる | can wait | つ → てる
- する → できる | can do | irregular
- {来|く}る → {来|こ}られる | can come | irregular
:::

The new verb is a normal る-verb, so you can make it negative or past: {話|はな}せない, {話|はな}せた.

## が instead of を

The thing you can do often takes が rather than を.

::: breakdown
ja: {私|わたし} は {漢字|かんじ} が {読|よ}める。
en: I can read kanji.
- {私|わたし}は = as for me
- {漢字|かんじ}が = kanji: what can be read
- {読|よ}める = can read
:::

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} は {来|こ}られません。
en: I can't come tomorrow.
:::

## {見|み}える and {聞|き}こえる

For things that are visible or audible by themselves, Japanese uses two special verbs.

::: example
ja: ここ から {富士山|ふじさん} が {見|み}える。
en: You can see Mount Fuji from here.
:::

::: example
ja: {音楽|おんがく} が {聞|き}こえる。
en: I can hear music.
:::

::: callout
title: ら-dropping
In speech, many people say {見|み}れる instead of {見|み}られる. You will hear it everywhere, but the full form is safer in writing.
:::

The て form plus いる describes a state that continues. It conjugates like any る-verb: ている, ていない, ていた.

## An action in progress

With verbs for ongoing actions, ている means *is doing*.

::: breakdown
ja: {今|いま} {何|なに} を して いる の?
en: What are you doing now?
- {今|いま} = now
- {何|なに}を = what
- している = are doing
:::

::: example
ja: {弟|おとうと} は {部屋|へや} で {寝|ね}て いる。
en: My younger brother is sleeping in his room.
:::

## A state that lasts

With verbs for a change that happens in an instant, ている means the change has happened and the result is still true.

::: example
ja: {電気|でんき} が ついて いる。
en: The light is on. (It came on, and it still is.)
:::

::: example
ja: {姉|あね} は {結婚|けっこん} して いる。
en: My older sister is married.
:::

::: callout
title: 知っている
"I know" is {知|し}って いる: you found out, and you still know. But "I don't know" is just {知|し}らない.
:::

## Dropping the い

In speech, the い often disappears: {見|み}ている becomes {見|み}てる. You will hear this all the time.

::: example
ja: {何|なに} {読|よ}んでる の?
en: What are you reading?
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {私|わたし} は {田中|たなか}さん を {知|し}る。
right: {私|わたし} は {田中|たなか}さん を {知|し}って いる。
why: For knowing someone now, use the ている form.
:::

- {食|た}べた いる
why: ている joins the て form of the verb.

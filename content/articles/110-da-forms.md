In the last lesson, だ declared that something is so. Japanese changes the ending of a word to change its meaning, so "is not" and "was" are not separate words. They are new endings on the same idea.

## The negative: じゃない

Swap だ for じゃない to say that something is *not* the case.

::: breakdown
ja: {学生|がくせい} じゃない。
en: (I'm) not a student.
- {学生|がくせい} = student
- じゃない = is not
:::

You will also see ではない. It means the same thing but sounds more careful, so it is common in writing.

## The past: だった

Swap だ for だった to talk about the past.

::: example
ja: {昨日|きのう} は {雨|あめ} だった。
en: Yesterday it was rain. (It rained yesterday.)
:::

## The past negative: じゃなかった

Take じゃない, drop the final い and add かった. This is the pattern you will see again and again: い becomes かった for the past.

::: example
ja: {友|とも}だち じゃなかった。
en: (He) wasn't a friend.
:::

## The whole table

::: vocab
- {学生|がくせい}だ | is a student | present
- {学生|がくせい}じゃない | is not a student | negative
- {学生|がくせい}だった | was a student | past
- {学生|がくせい}じゃなかった | was not a student | past negative
:::

::: callout
title: A pattern worth remembering
じゃない ends in い, and so does an entire class of adjectives you will meet soon. They all form the past the same way: ない becomes なかった. Learn it here once and it pays off for months.
:::

## In conversation

::: dialogue
Mai: {先生|せんせい}？ // (Are you) a teacher?
Tom: ううん、{先生|せんせい} じゃない。{学生|がくせい} だ。 // No, I'm not a teacher. I'm a student.
:::

ううん is a casual "no", and うん is a casual "yes". Be careful: they sound similar, but the falling-rising ううん is the no.

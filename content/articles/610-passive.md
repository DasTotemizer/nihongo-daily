## Making the passive form

- る-verbs: change る to られる. {食|た}べる becomes {食|た}べられる.
- う-verbs: move to the *a* column and add れる. {読|よ}む becomes {読|よ}まれる.
- する becomes される, and {来|く}る becomes {来|こ}られる.

For る-verbs, this looks exactly like the potential form. Context tells you which one it is.

## The basic passive

The person who did the action takes に.

::: breakdown
ja: {弟|おとうと} に ケーキ を {食|た}べられた。
en: My cake got eaten by my younger brother.
- {弟|おとうと}に = by my younger brother
- ケーキを = the cake
- {食|た}べられた = was eaten
:::

::: example
ja: この {本|ほん} は {世界|せかい} {中|じゅう} で {読|よ}まれて いる。
en: This book is read all over the world.
:::

## The suffering passive

Japanese can use the passive even with verbs that have no object, to show that something bothered you.

::: example
ja: {雨|あめ} に {降|ふ}られた。
en: I got caught in the rain.
:::

::: example
ja: {隣|となり} の {人|ひと} に タバコ を {吸|す}われた。
en: The person next to me smoked, and it bothered me.
:::

## Causative-passive: being made to do

Join the causative and the passive and you get *being made to do something*. る-verbs take させられる; う-verbs take せられる, often shortened to される.

::: example
ja: {子|こ}どもの {頃|ころ}、{野菜|やさい} を {食|た}べさせられた。
en: As a child, I was made to eat vegetables.
:::

::: example
ja: {一時間|いちじかん} も {待|ま}たされた。
en: I was made to wait a whole hour.
:::

::: callout
title: Passive as politeness
The passive form is also a light honorific. {先生|せんせい}が{来|こ}られた can mean *the teacher came*, said respectfully.
:::

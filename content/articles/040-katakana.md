Katakana is hiragana's angular twin. It has the same sounds, arranged in the same grid, but each character is made of straight strokes and sharp corners. Where hiragana is used for grammar, katakana is used mostly for words that came from other languages.

## The same grid, a new look

::: kana
title: The first rows of katakana
ア a | イ i | ウ u | エ e | オ o
カ ka | キ ki | ク ku | ケ ke | コ ko
サ sa | シ shi | ス su | セ se | ソ so
タ ta | チ chi | ツ tsu | テ te | ト to
ナ na | ニ ni | ヌ nu | ネ ne | ノ no
:::

::: kana
title: The rest of the grid
ハ ha | ヒ hi | フ fu | ヘ he | ホ ho
マ ma | ミ mi | ム mu | メ me | モ mo
ヤ ya | - | ユ yu | - | ヨ yo
ラ ra | リ ri | ル ru | レ re | ロ ro
ワ wa | - | - | - | ヲ o
ン n | - | - | - | -
:::

Everything you learnt in the last lesson still works: dashes for voiced sounds (ガ, ザ, ダ, バ), a circle for p (パ), small ャ ュ ョ, and a small ッ for a pause.

## The long dash

Katakana has one extra tool: a long line, ー, that stretches the vowel before it.

::: vocab
- コーヒー | coffee | noun
- ケーキ | cake | noun
- スーパー | supermarket | noun
- ラーメン | ramen | noun
:::

## Sounds Japanese did not have

To spell foreign sounds, katakana uses small vowels: ァ ィ ゥ ェ ォ. They combine with the character before them.

::: vocab
- ティー | tea, as in "tea bag" | noun
- フォーク | fork | noun
- パーティー | party | noun
:::

## Reading loanwords

English words change shape on their way into Japanese. Every consonant needs a vowel after it, and some sounds become their nearest Japanese neighbours. Saying them aloud is the quickest way to recognise them.

::: example
ja: テレビ
en: terebi: television (TV)
note: L and R become the same tapped r, and V usually becomes b.
:::

::: example
ja: クリスマス
en: kurisumasu: Christmas
:::

::: example
ja: アイスクリーム
en: aisukuriimu: ice cream
:::

::: callout
title: Look-alikes
シ (shi) and ツ (tsu) are the famous pair. In シ the short strokes stack on the left and the long stroke sweeps up; in ツ they sit along the top and the long stroke sweeps down. ソ (so) and ン (n) follow the same rule.
:::

Katakana is also used for foreign names, for animal and other sound effects, and sometimes for emphasis, the way English uses italics.

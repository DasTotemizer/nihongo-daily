You have met all three Japanese scripts. Here is what each one does, in a single page.

## Hiragana: the grammar script

Hiragana writes particles, verb endings and words with no common kanji. Every Japanese sentence uses it.

::: example
ja: これ は ねこ だ。
en: This is a cat.
:::

## The extras

Two small marks turn か into が and は into ば or ぱ. Small ゃ, ゅ and ょ blend sounds, as in きょう. A small っ doubles the next consonant, as in きって (stamp).

## Katakana: borrowed words

Katakana is mostly for words from other languages, and for names. The long dash ー stretches a sound.

::: example
ja: コーヒー と ケーキ
en: coffee and cake
:::

## Kanji: meaning at a glance

Kanji carry meaning, and most have more than one reading. Furigana show the reading until you are ready to go without.

::: example
ja: {山|やま} と {川|かわ}
en: mountains and rivers
:::

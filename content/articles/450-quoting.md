## Direct quotes

Put the exact words in 「」 brackets, then と and the verb.

::: example
ja: {田中|たなか}さん は 「おはよう」 と {言|い}った。
en: Mr Tanaka said, "Good morning."
:::

## Reported speech

Without the brackets, the quoted part is in the plain form. With a noun or な-adjective, keep だ.

::: breakdown
ja: {彼女|かのじょ} は {明日|あした} {来|こ}ない と {言|い}った。
en: She said she isn't coming tomorrow.
- {彼女|かのじょ}は = as for her
- {明日|あした}{来|こ}ない = not coming tomorrow: what she said
- と{言|い}った = she said
:::

## Thinking: と{思|おも}う

::: example
ja: あの {店|みせ} は {高|たか}い と {思|おも}う。
en: I think that shop is expensive.
:::

::: example
ja: {雨|あめ} だ と {思|おも}います。
en: I think it will rain.
:::

Volitional plus と{思|おも}う means *I'm thinking of doing*.

::: example
ja: {来年|らいねん}、{日本|にほん} に {住|す}もう と {思|おも}って いる。
en: I'm thinking of living in Japan next year.
:::

## って: the casual quote

In conversation, と becomes って. It can even stand in for the verb.

::: dialogue
A: {先生|せんせい}、{何|なん}て? // What did the teacher say?
B: {明日|あした} {休|やす}み だ って。 // She said there's no class tomorrow.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {学生|がくせい} と {言|い}った。
right: {学生|がくせい} だ と {言|い}った。
why: When you report a noun statement, keep だ before と.
:::

::: quiz
? choice quoting
q: How do you say "I think it's delicious"?
- おいしい と {思|おも}う *
- おいしい を {思|おも}う
- おいしい に {思|おも}う
why: Thoughts and words are quoted with と.

? choice quoting
q: What does {行|い}こう と {思|おも}う mean?
- I'm thinking of going. *
- I think he went.
- I must go.
why: Volitional plus と{思|おも}う means you're planning to do it.

? order quoting
q: Put the words in order: "He said he was busy."
words: {彼|かれ} は {忙|いそが}しい と {言|い}った
en: He said he was busy.
why: The quoted part comes before と, and the verb of saying comes last.
:::

## よう: it seems, judging by what I see

よう is a noun meaning *appearance*. Put it after a plain verb or adjective, after な-adjectives with な, and after nouns with の. End with だ or です.

::: example
ja: {誰|だれ} も いない よう だ。
en: It seems nobody is here.
:::

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} は {学生|がくせい} の よう です。
en: He seems to be a student.
:::

## みたい: the casual よう

みたい means the same as よう, but it is casual and simpler to use: nouns and な-adjectives take it directly.

::: example
ja: あの {人|ひと}、{先生|せんせい} みたい。
en: That person looks like a teacher.
:::

::: example
ja: {風邪|かぜ} を ひいた みたい。
en: I think I've caught a cold.
:::

## そう: it looks like, at a glance

For a guess based on how something looks right now, add そう to a verb stem, or to an adjective without its い or な.

::: vocab
- おいしそう | looks tasty | おいしい
- {高|たか}そう | looks expensive | {高|たか}い
- {元気|げんき}そう | looks well | {元気|げんき}な
- {雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}りそう | looks like it's going to rain | {降|ふ}る
:::

Two irregulars: いい becomes よさそう, and ない becomes なさそう.

::: example
ja: この ケーキ、おいしそう!
en: This cake looks delicious!
:::

::: mistake
wrong: このケーキ は おいしいそう だ。
right: このケーキ は おいしそう だ。
why: For appearance, drop the い before そう. おいしいそう means *I hear it's tasty*, which is next lesson.
:::

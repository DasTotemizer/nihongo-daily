In English we join ideas with *and*. Japanese does it by changing the first word into its **て form**. Only the last word in the sentence shows the tense.

## Nouns and な-adjectives: add で

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} は {学生|がくせい} で、{二十歳|はたち} だ。
en: He is a student, and he is twenty.
:::

::: example
ja: この {町|まち} は {静|しず}か で、きれい だ。
en: This town is quiet and pretty.
:::

## い-adjectives: い becomes くて

::: example
ja: この {店|みせ} は {安|やす}くて、おいしい。
en: This shop is cheap and tasty.
:::

## Verbs: just like the past tense

Take the past form and change た to て, or だ to で.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べた → {食|た}べて | eat | る-verb
- {飲|の}んだ → {飲|の}んで | drink | む → んで
- {行|い}った → {行|い}って | go | exception
- {買|か}った → {買|か}って | buy | う → って
- {書|か}いた → {書|か}いて | write | く → いて
- {話|はな}した → {話|はな}して | speak | す → して
- した → して | do | irregular
:::

With verbs, the て form usually means *and then*: the actions happen in order.

::: breakdown
ja: {朝|あさ} {起|お}きて、シャワー を {浴|あ}びて、{学校|がっこう} に {行|い}った。
en: I got up in the morning, had a shower and went to school.
- {起|お}きて = got up, and
- シャワーを{浴|あ}びて = had a shower, and
- {学校|がっこう}に{行|い}った = went to school: the past tense comes only here
:::

::: callout
title: The tense is at the end
Only the final word carries the tense or politeness. To make the sentence above polite, change just the last verb to {行|い}きました.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {安|やす}いで、おいしい。
right: {安|やす}くて、おいしい。
why: い-adjectives change い to くて. Only nouns and な-adjectives take で.
:::

::: quiz
? choice te-form
q: What is the て form of {飲|の}む?
- {飲|の}んで *
- {飲|の}みて
- {飲|の}って
why: It follows the past tense: {飲|の}んだ becomes {飲|の}んで.

? choice te-form
q: How do you join {高|たか}い and おいしい? (expensive and tasty)
- {高|たか}くて、おいしい *
- {高|たか}いで、おいしい
- {高|たか}いて、おいしい
why: い becomes くて.

? order te-form
q: Put the words in order: "I went home and slept."
words: {家|いえ} に {帰|かえ}って {寝|ね}た
en: I went home and slept.
why: The first action goes into the て form; the last verb shows the past tense.
:::

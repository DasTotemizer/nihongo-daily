## たい: want to do

Take the ます stem and add たい. The result works like an い-adjective.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べたい | want to eat | present
- {食|た}べたくない | don't want to eat | negative
- {食|た}べたかった | wanted to eat | past
- {食|た}べたくなかった | didn't want to eat | past negative
:::

::: breakdown
ja: {日本|にほん} に {行|い}きたい です。
en: I want to go to Japan.
- {日本|にほん}に = to Japan
- {行|い}きたい = want to go
- です = makes it polite
:::

The object of たい can take を or が.

::: example
ja: ラーメン が {食|た}べたい なあ。
en: I really want to eat ramen.
:::

## ほしい: want something

ほしい is an い-adjective. The thing you want takes が.

::: example
ja: {新|あたら}しい {靴|くつ} が ほしい。
en: I want new shoes.
:::

::: example
ja: {何|なに} も ほしくない。
en: I don't want anything.
:::

## Other people's wishes

Japanese doesn't guess at other people's feelings. For someone else, use たがる or ほしがる, which mean *shows signs of wanting*, usually in the ている form.

::: example
ja: {妹|いもうと} は {犬|いぬ} を ほしがって いる。
en: My younger sister wants a dog.
:::

::: callout
title: Asking what someone wants
Asking a superior {何|なに}がほしいですか can sound too direct. With guests, offer instead: コーヒーはいかがですか, would you like a coffee?
:::

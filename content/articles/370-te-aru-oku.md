## てある: it has been done

With a transitive verb, てある describes something someone did on purpose, which stays that way. The thing that was done takes が.

::: example
ja: {窓|まど} が {開|あ}けて ある。
en: The window has been opened. (Someone opened it, on purpose.)
:::

::: example
ja: {晩|ばん}ご{飯|はん} は もう {作|つく}って ある。
en: Dinner has already been made.
:::

Compare {窓|まど}が{開|あ}いている, which just says the window is open, with no hint of who did it or why.

## ておく: do it in advance

おく means *put*. After a て form, it means doing something now, ready for later.

::: example
ja: {旅行|りょこう} の {前|まえ} に、ホテル を {予約|よやく} して おく。
en: I'll book the hotel before the trip.
:::

In speech, ておく often shortens to とく: {買|か}っておく becomes {買|か}っとく.

## ていく and てくる: direction and change

いく means *go* and くる means *come*. After a て form, they show an action moving away or towards you, in space or in time.

::: example
ja: {傘|かさ} を {持|も}って いく。
en: I'll take an umbrella. (carry it as I go)
:::

::: example
ja: {友|とも}だち を {連|つ}れて くる。
en: I'll bring a friend. (lead them here)
:::

::: example
ja: だんだん {寒|さむ}く なって きた。
en: It has been getting colder and colder.
:::

::: callout
title: Time as distance
てくる describes change up to now. ていく describes change from now on: {人口|じんこう}が{減|へ}っていく, the population will go on falling.
:::

Think of giving as movement. Is the thing moving away from you, towards you, or are you the one receiving it?

## あげる: give, away from me

::: example
ja: {私|わたし} は {友|とも}だち に プレゼント を あげた。
en: I gave my friend a present.
:::

::: example
ja: {田中|たなか}さん は {鈴木|すずき}さん に {花|はな} を あげた。
en: Tanaka gave Suzuki flowers.
:::

## くれる: give, towards me

When someone gives to you, or to someone in your group such as your family, use くれる.

::: example
ja: {母|はは} が {時計|とけい} を くれた。
en: My mother gave me a watch.
:::

## もらう: receive

With もらう, the receiver is the subject, and the giver takes に or から.

::: breakdown
ja: {私|わたし} は {先生|せんせい} に {本|ほん} を もらった。
en: I got a book from my teacher.
- {私|わたし}は = as for me: the receiver
- {先生|せんせい}に = from the teacher
- {本|ほん}を = a book
- もらった = received
:::

::: callout
title: The one rule
Nobody uses あげる for something given to them. If it comes your way, it's くれる.
:::

## Favours with the て form

Attach these verbs to a て form and they describe doing something for someone.

::: example
ja: {友|とも}だち が {駅|えき} まで {送|おく}って くれた。
en: My friend kindly drove me to the station.
:::

::: example
ja: {先輩|せんぱい} に {宿題|しゅくだい} を {手伝|てつだ}って もらった。
en: I got a senior student to help me with my homework.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {友|とも}だち が {私|わたし} に ケーキ を あげた。
right: {友|とも}だち が {私|わたし} に ケーキ を くれた。
why: The cake came towards you, so the verb is くれる.
:::

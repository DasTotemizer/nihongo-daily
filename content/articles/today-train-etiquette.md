Tokyo's trains carry millions of people a day, yet a carriage at rush hour can be almost silent. That calm comes from a handful of shared habits.

## The unwritten rules

- Phones go on silent, and people avoid calls on board.
- Conversations are kept quiet.
- Queue at the marked spots on the platform, and let people off first.
- Wear your backpack on your front in a crowded train.
- Priority seats near the doors are for older people, pregnant passengers and anyone who needs them.

## Kanji on the signs

::: vocab
- {電車|でんしゃ} | train | noun
- {駅|えき} | station | noun
- {入口|いりぐち} | entrance | sign
- {出口|でぐち} | exit | sign
- {東口|ひがしぐち} | east exit | sign
- {乗|の}り{場|ば} | platform, boarding area | sign
- {優先席|ゆうせんせき} | priority seat | sign
:::

{口|くち} means mouth. An exit is an "out mouth", {出口|でぐち}, and an entrance is an "in mouth", {入口|いりぐち}. Big stations have many: {東口|ひがしぐち} (east), {西口|にしぐち} (west), {南口|みなみぐち} (south) and {北口|きたぐち} (north).

::: callout
title: Meeting a friend
Arranging to meet at a big station? Name the exit, not just the station. {新宿駅|しんじゅくえき} has over two hundred exits.
:::

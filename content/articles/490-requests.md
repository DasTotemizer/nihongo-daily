## ください: please

After a noun with を, ください means *please give me*. After a て form, it means *please do*.

::: example
ja: {水|みず} を ください。
en: Water, please.
:::

::: example
ja: ちょっと {待|ま}って ください。
en: Please wait a moment.
:::

For *please don't*, use ないで ください.

::: example
ja: ここ に {止|と}めないで ください。
en: Please don't park here.
:::

## Politer: くれる and もらう as questions

Asking whether someone will do you a favour is politer than telling them to.

::: vocab
- {待|ま}って くれる? | will you wait? | casual
- {待|ま}って くれません か | won't you wait for me? | polite
- {待|ま}って もらえません か | could I have you wait? | more polite
- {待|ま}って いただけません か | might I ask you to wait? | very polite
:::

## Casual: ちょうだい and the bare て form

With friends, ください becomes ちょうだい, or just the て form on its own.

::: example
ja: それ、{取|と}って。
en: Pass me that.
:::

## なさい: a firm instruction

The ます stem plus なさい is what parents and teachers say.

::: example
ja: {早|はや}く {寝|ね}なさい。
en: Go to bed now.
:::

## The command form

The plain command form is very rough. You will see it on signs and hear it in sports and anime. る-verbs change る to ろ; う-verbs move to the *e* column.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べろ | eat! | る-verb
- {行|い}け | go! | う-verb
- しろ | do it! | する
- {来|こ}い | come! | {来|く}る
- {行|い}くな | don't go! | negative command
:::

q: How do you say "Please don't run"?

- {走|はし}って ください
- {走|はし}るな ください

- {教|おし}えて

- {飲|の}もう

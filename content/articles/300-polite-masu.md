Japanese changes with who you are talking to. With friends and family, plain forms like {食|た}べる are fine. With people you do not know well, you add politeness with ます.

## Making the ます stem

- る-verbs: drop る. {食|た}べる becomes {食|た}べ.
- う-verbs: move the last sound to the *i* column. {飲|の}む becomes {飲|の}み.
- する becomes し, and {来|く}る becomes {来|き}.

Then add ます.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べる → {食|た}べます | eat | る-verb
- {飲|の}む → {飲|の}みます | drink | む → み
- {行|い}く → {行|い}きます | go | く → き
- {話|はな}す → {話|はな}します | speak | す → し
- {買|か}う → {買|か}います | buy | う → い
- する → します | do | irregular
- {来|く}る → {来|き}ます | come | irregular
:::

## The four endings

Once you have the stem, there are just four endings to learn, and they are the same for every verb.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べます | eat | ます
- {食|た}べません | don't eat | ません
- {食|た}べました | ate | ました
- {食|た}べませんでした | didn't eat | ませんでした
:::

::: breakdown
ja: {毎朝|まいあさ} コーヒー を {飲|の}みます。
en: I drink coffee every morning.
- {毎朝|まいあさ} = every morning
- コーヒーを = coffee: the object
- {飲|の}みます = drink, politely
:::

::: example
ja: {昨日|きのう} {学校|がっこう} に {行|い}きませんでした。
en: I didn't go to school yesterday.
:::

::: dialogue
{店員|てんいん}: カード を {使|つか}います か。 // Will you use a card?
{客|きゃく}: いいえ、{現金|げんきん} で {払|はら}います。 // No, I'll pay in cash.
:::

::: callout
title: Which should you use?
When unsure, use ます. Being too polite is fine. Being too casual with a stranger can sound rude.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {飲|の}むます
right: {飲|の}みます
why: ます joins the stem, not the dictionary form. む changes to み first.
:::

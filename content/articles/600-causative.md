## Making the causative form

- る-verbs: change る to させる. {食|た}べる becomes {食|た}べさせる.
- う-verbs: move to the *a* column and add せる. {行|い}く becomes {行|い}かせる; う becomes わ, so {買|か}う becomes {買|か}わせる.
- する becomes させる, and {来|く}る becomes {来|こ}させる.

The result is a normal る-verb.

::: vocab
- {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}たせる | make or let wait | つ → たせる
- {飲|の}む → {飲|の}ませる | make or let drink | む → ませる
- {読|よ}む → {読|よ}ませる | make or let read | む → ませる
- する → させる | make or let do | irregular
:::

## Make or let?

The person who is made or allowed to act takes に (or を with verbs like *go*). Context tells you whether it's force or permission.

::: breakdown
ja: {先生|せんせい} は {学生|がくせい} に {本|ほん} を {読|よ}ませた。
en: The teacher made the students read the book.
- {先生|せんせい}は = the teacher: the one causing it
- {学生|がくせい}に = the students: the ones who act
- {読|よ}ませた = made read
:::

::: example
ja: {子|こ}ども を {公園|こうえん} で {遊|あそ}ばせた。
en: I let the children play in the park.
:::

::: example
ja: {待|ま}たせて すみません。
en: Sorry to have kept you waiting.
:::

## させてください: let me

The causative plus て ください asks for permission to do something yourself. It is a polite way to offer.

::: example
ja: {私|わたし} に {払|はら}わせて ください。
en: Please let me pay.
:::

::: callout
title: A shorter version
In speech you will also hear a short causative: {食|た}べさす, {行|い}かす. It means the same thing.
:::

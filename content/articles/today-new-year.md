Christmas in Japan is a fun, romantic evening. The New Year, お{正月|しょうがつ}, is the family holiday. Many people travel home, and shops and offices close for several days.

## New Year's Eve

On {大晦日|おおみそか}, the last night of the year, families eat soba noodles for a long life. At midnight, temple bells ring 108 times, one for each human worry in Buddhist teaching.

## The first days

- **{初詣|はつもうで}**: the first shrine or temple visit of the year, to pray for good luck.
- **おせち**: boxes of colourful foods, each with a meaning. Prawns for a long life, black beans for good health.
- **{年賀状|ねんがじょう}**: New Year cards, delivered on the morning of 1 January.
- **お{年玉|としだま}**: envelopes of money for children.

## What people say

::: vocab
- あけまして おめでとう ございます | Happy New Year | after midnight
- よいお{年|とし}を | have a good New Year | before the New Year
- ことし も よろしく お{願|ねが}いします | I look forward to this year with you | to friends and colleagues
:::

::: callout
title: Don't say it too early
あけまして means "the year has opened". Say it only from 1 January. Before then, wish people よいお{年|とし}を.
:::

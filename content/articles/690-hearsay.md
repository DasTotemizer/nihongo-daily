## そうだ: I hear that

For hearsay, add そうだ to the full plain form, with nothing removed. Nouns and な-adjectives keep だ.

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} は {雪|ゆき} だ そう です。
en: I hear it's going to snow tomorrow.
:::

::: example
ja: あの {店|みせ} は おいしい そう だ。
en: I hear that shop is good.
:::

::: callout
title: Two kinds of そう
おいしそう (looks tasty) drops the い. おいしいそう (I hear it's tasty) keeps it. That small difference changes the meaning.
:::

## らしい: apparently

らしい is like そうだ but less direct: it is a conclusion drawn from what you have heard or seen. Nouns and な-adjectives take it directly.

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} は {会社|かいしゃ} を やめた らしい。
en: Apparently he quit his job.
:::

After a noun, らしい can also mean *typical of*.

::: example
ja: {今日|きょう} は {春|はる}らしい {天気|てんき} だ。
en: Today's weather is very spring-like.
:::

## っぽい: -ish

っぽい is casual. It means *seems like* or *has a feel of*.

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} の {話|はなし} は {嘘|うそ}っぽい。
en: His story sounds a bit like a lie.
:::

::: example
ja: {子|こ}どもっぽい。
en: That's childish.
:::

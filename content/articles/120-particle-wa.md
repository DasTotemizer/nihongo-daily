One of the first things you meet in Japanese is the particle は. It is written with the hiragana for *ha*, but when it works as a particle it is always pronounced **wa**. That spelling is a leftover from older Japanese, and every learner trips over it once.

So what does it do? In short, は announces the topic: the thing you are about to say something about. Think of it as saying "As for…" before you get to the point.

## As for me…

Here is the sentence almost every learner says first.

::: breakdown
ja: {私|わたし} は {学生|がくせい} だ。
en: I am a student.
- {私|わたし} = I, me
- は = topic marker: "as for…", said *wa*
- {学生|がくせい} = student
- だ = is, am, are (casual)
:::

Read literally, it says "As for me, student is." Japanese puts the topic first, then the comment about it, and finishes with だ to say "that's how it is". You learnt だ in the previous lesson; は simply tells the listener what the だ is about.

::: example
ja: {田中|たなか}さん は {先生|せんせい} だ。
en: Mr Tanaka is a teacher.
note: さん is a polite title that goes after a name. Never use it for yourself.
:::

## The topic does not have to be the subject

This is where は differs from anything in English. The topic is not always the person doing something. It can be almost anything you want to talk about, and the rest of the sentence comments on it.

::: example
ja: {今日|きょう} は {雨|あめ} だ。
en: Today, it's rain. (It's raining today.)
note: Nobody is claiming that today *is* rain. {今日|きょう} is just the topic: as for today, rain.
:::

::: example
ja: {明日|あした} は {休|やす}み だ。
en: Tomorrow is a day off.
:::

## Topics carry over

Once a topic is set, Japanese happily leaves it out of the next sentences. Both speakers know what is being discussed.

::: dialogue
Aki: アリス さん は {学生|がくせい}？ // Alice, are you a student?
Alice: うん、{学生|がくせい} だ。 // Yeah, I'm a student.
:::

Alice does not repeat {私|わたし}_は. The topic is already on the table, so the shortest natural answer is just {学生|がくせい}_だ. Repeating {私|わたし}_は in every sentence is one of the quickest ways to sound like a textbook.

::: callout
title: Casual questions
In casual speech a question is often just a statement said with a rising voice, and だ is dropped. You will learn the polite question marker か in Stage 3.
:::

## A common mistake

::: mistake
wrong: {私|わたし} わ {学生|がくせい} だ。
right: {私|わたし} は {学生|がくせい} だ。
why: You say *wa*, but you always write は. わ is a different word entirely.
:::

## What to remember

- は marks the **topic**: what you are talking about.
- It is written は and said **wa**.
- The topic can be a person, a time, a place or almost anything else.
- Once the topic is clear, you can leave it out.

In the next lessons you will meet も, which works like は but means "also", and が, which picks out one particular thing. The difference between は and が is famous; take it one step at a time.

## だけ: only

だけ goes after the thing that is limited.

::: example
ja: {水|みず} だけ ください。
en: Just water, please.
:::

## しか～ない: nothing but

しか also means *only*, but the verb must be negative. It stresses that it isn't enough.

::: breakdown
ja: お{金|かね} が {百円|ひゃくえん} しか ない。
en: I only have a hundred yen.
- {百円|ひゃくえん}しか = nothing but 100 yen
- ない = there isn't: required with しか
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {一人|ひとり} しか {来|き}た。
right: {一人|ひとり} しか {来|こ}なかった。
why: しか always needs a negative verb.
:::

## ばかり: nothing but, all the time

::: example
ja: {弟|おとうと} は ゲーム ばかり して いる。
en: My younger brother does nothing but play games.
:::

## すぎる: too much

Add すぎる to a verb stem or an adjective without its い or な.

::: vocab
- {食|た}べすぎる | eat too much | verb stem
- {高|たか}すぎる | too expensive | い-adjective
- {静|しず}かすぎる | too quiet | な-adjective
:::

::: example
ja: {昨日|きのう} は {飲|の}みすぎた。
en: I drank too much yesterday.
:::

## ほど: to the extent that

ほど compares or sets a limit. With a negative, it means *not as much as*.

::: example
ja: {今日|きょう} は {昨日|きのう} ほど {寒|さむ}くない。
en: Today isn't as cold as yesterday.
:::

## さ: turning adjectives into nouns

Swap い or な for さ to talk about the amount of a quality.

::: example
ja: この {山|やま} の {高|たか}さ は {三千|さんぜん} メートル だ。
en: The height of this mountain is 3,000 metres.
:::

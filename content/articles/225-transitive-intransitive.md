In English, "open" works both ways: *I open the door* and *the door opens*. Japanese usually uses two different verbs for these two ideas.

- A **transitive** verb is something someone does to an object. The object takes を.
- An **intransitive** verb is something that happens by itself. The thing that changes takes が.

## Common pairs

::: vocab
- {開|あ}ける / {開|あ}く | open (something) / (something) opens | transitive / intransitive
- {閉|し}める / {閉|し}まる | close (something) / closes | transitive / intransitive
- {入|い}れる / {入|はい}る | put in / enter | transitive / intransitive
- {出|だ}す / {出|で}る | take out / come out | transitive / intransitive
- つける / つく | turn on / comes on | transitive / intransitive
- {消|け}す / {消|き}える | turn off / goes off | transitive / intransitive
- {落|お}とす / {落|お}ちる | drop / fall | transitive / intransitive
:::

## The particle gives it away

::: breakdown
ja: {私|わたし} が ドア を {開|あ}けた。
en: I opened the door.
- {私|わたし}が = I: the one doing it
- ドアを = the door: the object
- {開|あ}けた = opened (something)
:::

::: breakdown
ja: ドア が {開|あ}いた。
en: The door opened.
- ドアが = the door: the thing that changed
- {開|あ}いた = opened (by itself)
:::

Notice that the second sentence has no person in it at all. That is the point of an intransitive verb: it tells you what happened, not who did it.

::: example
ja: {電気|でんき} を {消|け}した。
en: I turned off the light.
:::

::: example
ja: {電気|でんき} が {消|き}えた。
en: The light went off.
:::

::: callout
title: Why it matters
Japanese speakers often choose the intransitive verb to sound less blunt. Saying a cup *fell* is softer than saying you *dropped* it, even when you did.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: ドア を {開|あ}いた。
right: ドア を {開|あ}けた。
why: を marks an object, so it needs the transitive verb {開|あ}ける. With {開|あ}く, the door takes が.
:::

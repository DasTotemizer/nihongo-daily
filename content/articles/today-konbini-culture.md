The word コンビニ is short for コンビニエンスストア, convenience store. Japanese often shortens long borrowed words like this. In Japan, the konbini is part of daily life: open all day and night, on nearly every street.

## What you can do there

- Buy a hot lunch, a rice ball or a coffee from the machine.
- Pay utility bills and buy concert tickets.
- Send and collect parcels.
- Print documents and photos.
- Withdraw cash from the ATM, which often accepts foreign cards.

## What to try

::: vocab
- おにぎり | rice ball | snack
- お{弁当|べんとう} | boxed meal | lunch
- サンドイッチ | sandwich | snack
- からあげ | fried chicken | hot food
- アイス | ice cream | dessert
:::

おにぎり come with fillings like salmon, tuna and mayonnaise, or pickled plum. The wrapper has numbered tabs that keep the seaweed crisp. Pull 1, then 2 and 3, and the rice ball comes out ready to eat.

::: callout
title: Shortened words
コンビニ (convenience store), スマホ (smartphone) and パソコン (personal computer) are all clipped from longer English words. Spotting the original is a fun puzzle.
:::

## Manners

Eating while walking is not forbidden, but many people eat outside the shop or take food home. Bins are rare in the street, so people often use the bins by the konbini door.

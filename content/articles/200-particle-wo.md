When a verb acts on something, Japanese marks that something with を. Grammar books call it the direct object. In "I eat sushi", sushi is the direct object: it is the thing being eaten.

を is written with the hiragana once read *wo*, but today it is pronounced simply **o**.

## Object, を, verb

::: breakdown
ja: すし を {食|た}べる。
en: (I) eat sushi.
- すし = sushi
- を = marks the object: what is eaten
- {食|た}べる = eat
:::

::: example
ja: {毎日|まいにち} コーヒー を {飲|の}む。
en: (I) drink coffee every day.
note: {毎日|まいにち} means every day. Time words like this usually need no particle.
:::

::: example
ja: {昨日|きのう} {映画|えいが} を {見|み}た。
en: (I) watched a film yesterday.
:::

## Moving through a place

を has a second use that surprises English speakers. With verbs of movement, it marks the space you move *through* or *along*.

::: example
ja: {公園|こうえん} を {歩|ある}く。
en: (I) walk through the park.
:::

::: example
ja: {道|みち} を {渡|わた}る。
en: (I) cross the road.
:::

## を with する

Many activities are a noun plus する. を can join them, which makes the noun the object of "do".

::: example
ja: {毎晩|まいばん} {勉強|べんきょう} を する。
en: (I) study every evening.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: すし を だ。
right: すし だ。
why: を only works with verbs that act on something. Nouns with だ never take を.
:::

## が and けど: but

Add が or けど to the end of a clause to say *but*. が sounds more formal; けど is everyday speech.

::: example
ja: {日本語|にほんご} は {難|むずか}しい けど、{面白|おもしろ}い。
en: Japanese is hard, but it's interesting.
:::

::: example
ja: {行|い}きたい です が、{時間|じかん} が ありません。
en: I'd like to go, but I don't have time.
:::

With a noun or な-adjective, add だ first: {学生|がくせい}だけど.

## Soft endings

Japanese often stops at けど or が without finishing the sentence. It softens a request or leaves room for the listener to reply.

::: dialogue
A: すみません、{駅|えき} に {行|い}きたい ん です けど…… // Excuse me, I want to get to the station, but...
B: あ、あそこ です よ。 // Oh, it's over there.
:::

## たり: doing things like this and that

To list a few actions as examples, put each verb in its past form, add り, and finish with する.

::: breakdown
ja: {週末|しゅうまつ} は {映画|えいが} を {見|み}たり、{本|ほん} を {読|よ}んだり する。
en: At weekends I do things like watch films and read books.
- {見|み}たり = watching, among other things
- {読|よ}んだり = reading, among other things
- する = I do: this carries the tense
:::

::: callout
title: Not the whole list
たり says *things like*. If you want to list everything in order, use the て form instead.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: {本|ほん} を {読|よ}んだり、テレビ を {見|み}る。
right: {本|ほん} を {読|よ}んだり、テレビ を {見|み}たり する。
why: Every item in the list takes たり, and the sentence ends with する.
:::

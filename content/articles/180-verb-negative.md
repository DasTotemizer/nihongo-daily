To say you do not do something, Japanese changes the end of the verb to ない. You have seen ない before, in じゃない. The method depends on the verb's group, which is why the last lesson mattered.

## る-verbs: drop る, add ない

::: example
ja: {食|た}べる → {食|た}べない
en: eat → don't eat
:::

::: example
ja: {見|み}る → {見|み}ない
en: see → don't see
:::

## う-verbs: change the last sound to its a-version

Take the final *u* sound and move it to the *a* column of the kana chart, then add ない.

::: vocab
- {飲|の}む → {飲|の}まない | don't drink | む → ま
- {話|はな}す → {話|はな}さない | don't speak | す → さ
- {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}たない | don't wait | つ → た
- {行|い}く → {行|い}かない | don't go | く → か
- {帰|かえ}る → {帰|かえ}らない | don't go home | る → ら
:::

## One special case: う becomes わ

Verbs ending in う do not use あ. They use わ instead.

::: example
ja: {買|か}う → {買|か}わない
en: buy → don't buy
:::

## The irregulars, and ある

::: vocab
- する → しない | don't do | irregular
- {来|く}る → {来|こ}ない | don't come | irregular: the reading changes too
- ある → ない | there isn't | exception
:::

ある means "there is" for objects. Its negative is simply ない, with nothing in front.

::: breakdown
ja: お{金|かね} が ない。
en: (I) have no money. (There's no money.)
- お{金|かね} = money
- が = marks what there is, or isn't
- ない = there isn't: the negative of ある
:::

::: callout
title: Negative verbs are い-adjectives
Every ない form ends in い, and conjugates exactly like an い-adjective. That is why the past of {食|た}べない will be {食|た}べなかった, as you will see next.
:::

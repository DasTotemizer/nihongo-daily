Japanese has two kinds of adjectives. The first kind is called the **な-adjective**, because it adds な when it sits in front of a noun. The good news is that you already know how it conjugates: exactly like a noun with だ.

## Before a noun: add な

::: breakdown
ja: {静|しず}か な {町|まち}
en: a quiet town
- {静|しず}か = quiet
- な = links the adjective to the noun
- {町|まち} = town
:::

::: example
ja: きれい な {花|はな}
en: a pretty flower
:::

::: example
ja: {有名|ゆうめい} な {人|ひと}
en: a famous person
:::

## At the end of a sentence: use だ

When the adjective is the comment of a sentence, it takes the same endings as a noun.

::: example
ja: この {町|まち} は {静|しず}か だ。
en: This town is quiet.
note: この means "this", and goes right before a noun.
:::

::: vocab
- {静|しず}かだ | is quiet | present
- {静|しず}かじゃない | is not quiet | negative
- {静|しず}かだった | was quiet | past
- {静|しず}かじゃなかった | was not quiet | past negative
:::

## {好|す}き: the adjective of liking

The way to say you like something in Japanese is an adjective, {好|す}き. The thing you like is marked with が.

::: breakdown
ja: {私|わたし} は {魚|さかな} が {好|す}き だ。
en: I like fish.
- {私|わたし}は = as for me
- {魚|さかな}が = fish (the thing that is liked)
- {好|す}きだ = is liked
:::

Literally: "As for me, fish is liked." It sounds odd in English, but it is perfectly natural Japanese, and it uses both は and が in their proper jobs.

::: example
ja: {肉|にく} は {好|す}き じゃない。
en: I don't like meat.
note: Here {肉|にく} becomes the topic with は, which often happens in negative sentences.
:::

::: mistake
wrong: きれい {花|はな}
right: きれい な {花|はな}
why: な-adjectives need な before a noun. Without it, the two words do not connect.
:::

::: callout
title: Watch out for きれい
きれい ends in い, so it looks like the other adjective type. It is not. It is a な-adjective: きれいな{花|はな}, きれいじゃない. A few others like this are きらい (disliked) and ゆうめい (famous).
:::

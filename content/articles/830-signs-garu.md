## がる: showing a feeling

Japanese doesn't state other people's feelings directly. Add がる to an adjective without its い or な, and it describes how they act.

::: vocab
- {寒|さむ}がる | act cold, feel the cold | {寒|さむ}い
- {怖|こわ}がる | be scared | {怖|こわ}い
- {嫌|いや}がる | show dislike | {嫌|いや}な
- {恥|は}ずかしがる | act shy | {恥|は}ずかしい
:::

::: example
ja: {息子|むすこ} は {犬|いぬ} を {怖|こわ}がって いる。
en: My son is scared of dogs.
:::

::: example
ja: {恥|は}ずかしがらないで。
en: Don't be shy.
:::

## ばかり: as if to say

The ない form plus ばかり(に) describes someone acting as if they were about to say something.

::: example
ja: {彼|かれ} は {帰|かえ}れ と {言|い}わん ばかり の {顔|かお} を した。
en: He gave me a look as if to say "Go home".
:::

## めく: showing hints of

Add めく to a noun to say something is starting to show that quality.

::: example
ja: {最近|さいきん}、{春|はる}めいて きた。
en: Lately it's started to feel like spring.
:::

::: example
ja: {謎|なぞ}めいた {笑顔|えがお}
en: a mysterious smile, a smile with a hint of mystery
:::
